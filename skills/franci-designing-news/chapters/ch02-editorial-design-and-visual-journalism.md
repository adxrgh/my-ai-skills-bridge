# Chapter 2: Editorial Design and Visual Journalism

## Core Idea
Visual journalism fuses journalistic reporting with visual design. The editorial designer is a co-author of the news story who uses typography, data visualization, spatial pacing, and page furniture to explain complex reality clearly and engagingly.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Co-Author Newsroom Model**:
  - When to use: Structuring editorial workflows between journalists, editors, and art directors.
  - How: Embed designers from the inception of a story:
    1. *Story Conception*: Journalist and art director brainstorm visual narrative entry points together.
    2. *Data & Diagramming*: The designer investigates raw data alongside reporters to uncover hidden patterns.
    3. *Modular Assembly*: The story is designed as an integrated spread combining text, data visualization, photos, and sidebars.
- **Modular Storytelling Architecture**:
  - When to use: Layout design for complex investigative reports, magazine features, or news explainers.
  - How: Deconstruct a long narrative into six integrated visual layers:
    - *Main Narrative*: Core journalistic text column.
    - *Hero Visual*: High-impact anchor photo, illustration, or lead infographic.
    - *Data Callout*: Big stat numbers highlighting key metrics.
    - *Explanatory Diagram*: Process chart or relational map explaining *how* something works.
    - *Timeline / Sidebar*: Chronological context or secondary background profiles.
    - *Page Furniture*: Category tags, folios, running headers, and section rules.

## Key Concepts
- **Visual Journalism**: Communicating news narrative through the intentional synthesis of typography, graphic diagrams, photography, and layout structure.
- **Page Furniture**: Functional navigation elements (folios, section headers, category labels, bylines, date stamps) that guide readers through a publication.
- **Modular Layout**: Organizing a page or spread into distinct spatial blocks that can be read independently or sequentially.
- **Entry Points**: High-contrast visual anchors (headlines, pull quotes, big numbers, infographics) that hook scanning readers into a story.

## Mental Models
- **Spread as Exhibition Wall**: Think of a double-page spread or long web feature as a curated exhibition wall where text, photography, and data visualizations converse.
- **The 3-Second Hook Rule**: A visual entry point must communicate the story's core thesis in under 3 seconds.

## Anti-patterns
- **Silod Text-Then-Design Workflow**: Writing the entire text in isolation and handing it to a designer at the last minute to "make it look pretty."
- **Visual Homogeneity**: Making every article layout look identical, removing contrast between breaking news, opinions, and deep features.

## Reference Tables

### Modular News Component Architecture

| Component | Primary Function | Ideal Typographic Treatment | Placement Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headline (H1)** | Primary story title & hook | Bold Display Serif or Sans ($>36\text{pt}$) | Top anchor of feature block |
| **Stand-First (Deck)** | Story summary & context | Medium weight, $1.3\times$ body size | Immediately under headline |
| **Data Callout** | Key numerical statistic | Giant bold digits ($>48\text{pt}$) + small label | Flanking text column in sidebar |
| **Infographic Diagram** | Process / relational explainer | Clean, low-contrast sans-serif captions | Centered or spanning 2 columns |
| **Sidebar / Bio** | Secondary context or timeline | Muted background fill + smaller text | Right or bottom margin block |

## Worked Example

### Designing a Modular News Feature Spread
1. **Raw Input**: A 2,000-word investigative text about global supply chain disruptions.
2. **Deconstruction**:
   - Extract 300 words of timeline milestones into a **Chronological Sidebar**.
   - Extract key statistics into 3 **Big Data Callouts** (e.g., "$4.2B", "+140%", "18 Days").
   - Create a **Flow Diagram** showing shipping port bottlenecks.
3. **Spread Assembly**: Place the main text in a clean 3-column block on the right page; anchor the left page with the Flow Diagram and Big Data Callouts.
4. **Result**: The story becomes scannable, engaging, and deeply informative.

## Key Takeaways
1. Visual journalism makes complex stories accessible through structural design.
2. Embed art directors and designers early in the story reporting process.
3. Use modular page elements (callouts, diagrams, timelines) to create multiple entry points.
4. Page furniture provides essential navigation scaffolding across publications.
