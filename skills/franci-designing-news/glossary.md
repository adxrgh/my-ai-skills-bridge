# Glossary of Editorial & Visual Journalism Terms

Alphabetical dictionary of key terms, concepts, and technical definitions from *Designing News* by Francesco Franci.

---

- **Affordance** — Visual cues (color shifts, underlined text, button borders) signaling interactivity in digital interfaces (Ch 05).
- **Anatomical Cutaway** — An explanatory graphic revealing internal structural components of an object or building (Ch 04).
- **Berliner Format** — A compact newspaper size ($470 \times 315\text{ mm}$) popularized by *The Guardian* as a middle ground between broadsheet and tabloid (Ch 03, Ch 06).
- **Bespoke Type Commissioning** — Designing custom font super-families tailored specifically to a publication's technical constraints and brand voice (Ch 03, Ch 06).
- **Brand Equity** — The accrued visual recognition, trust, and emotional connection readers associate with a publication's masthead and layout style (Ch 03).
- **Chartjunk** — Unnecessary decorative elements (3D effects, drop shadows, fake textures) that obscure or distort data readability (Ch 04).
- **Continuous News Cycle** — The 24/7 digital publishing stream that replaced once-a-day print deadlines (Ch 01).
- **Data Callout** — A key numerical statistic set in large bold typography to highlight data points in an article (Ch 02, Ch 04).
- **Data Visualization** — Transforming raw datasets into clear visual geometries (length, area, position, color) to reveal patterns (Ch 04).
- **Deck (Stand-first)** — A brief introductory paragraph positioned between headline and body copy summarizing the story (Ch 02).
- **Entry Point** — High-contrast visual anchors (headlines, pull quotes, big numbers, infographics) that hook scanning readers into a story (Ch 02).
- **Flowchart** — A sequential diagram explaining step-by-step how a process, law, or mechanism operates (Ch 04).
- **Guardian Egyptian** — The iconic 96-weight bespoke font super-family designed by Commercial Type for *The Guardian* (Ch 03, Ch 06).
- **IL (Intelligence in Lifestyle)** — Italian magazine directed by Francesco Franci known for pioneering infographic-driven editorial design (Ch 04, Ch 06).
- **Information Architecture** — The structural organization of complex news data and navigation pathways to make information scannable and intelligible (Ch 01).
- **Infographic Thinking** — The analytical journalism habit of translating complex news data into structured, self-explanatory visual maps (Ch 01, Ch 04).
- **Liquid / Responsive Grid** — Layout frameworks that dynamically adapt column counts, font sizes, and margins to fit any screen width (Ch 05).
- **Macro/Micro Reading Architecture** — Designing graphics or spreads that offer a high-level summary at a glance (macro) while revealing granular details upon close inspection (micro) (Ch 04).
- **Masthead** — The iconic banner or logo title of a newspaper or magazine positioned at the top of the cover (Ch 03).
- **Modular Storytelling Architecture** — Deconstructing long narrative articles into integrated visual layers (main text, data callouts, diagrams, sidebars) (Ch 02).
- **Network Diagram (Relational Map)** — A node-link diagram showing connections between people, companies, or concepts (Ch 04).
- **Orientation Shift** — Layouts that dynamically reconfigure when a tablet is rotated between portrait (vertical 1-col) and landscape (horizontal multi-col) (Ch 05).
- **Page Furniture** — Functional navigation elements (folios, section headers, category labels, bylines, date stamps) guiding readers through a publication (Ch 02).
- **Pull Quote** — A key sentence extracted from main text and set in a dramatic large font to hook scanning readers (Ch 02).
- **Redesign** — A systematic structural overhaul of a publication's visual identity, spatial grid, typography, and content organization (Ch 03).
- **Scrollytelling** — Digital multimedia storytelling where interactive visuals (video loops, 3D maps, chart animations) trigger in sync with page scrolling (Ch 05, Ch 06).
- **Semantic Color Coding** — Using color strictly to categorize or quantify data, avoiding decorative or arbitrary color choices (Ch 04).
- **Snow Fall** — The landmark 2012 New York Times digital feature directed by Steve Duenes that defined modern scrollytelling (Ch 05, Ch 06).
- **Visual Journalism** — Treating design, typography, data visualization, and layout as core reporting methodologies rather than post-writing decoration (Ch 01, Ch 02).
