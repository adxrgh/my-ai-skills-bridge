# Chapter 1: Editorial Design

## Core Idea
Editorial design is the art and process of synthesizing words, images, and visual hierarchy across physical and digital platforms to inform, entertain, and engage a target audience. It bridges journalism, storytelling, and visual strategy, managing complex editorial workflows across print, web, app, and tablet formats.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Tri-Platform Convergence Model**:
  - When to use: When developing an editorial publication across print, web, and mobile/tablet platforms.
  - How: Define core brand essence first, then adapt layout mechanics per format:
    - *Print*: Fixed format, tactile experience, tactile pacing, high-resolution physical spreads.
    - *Web*: Continuous scroll, responsive breakpoints, real-time analytics, instant engagement.
    - *Tablet/App*: Touch interactivity, motion/video integration, orientation shifts (portrait vs. landscape).
- **The Editorial Staff Matrix (Roles & Responsibilities)**:
  - When to use: Structuring an editorial art department or establishing workflow boundaries between editorial and design.
  - How: Assign clear ownership across 6 core roles:
    1. *Editor-in-Chief / Editor*: Defines voice, content direction, and final sign-off.
    2. *Creative Director / Art Director*: Establishes visual identity, typography rules, grid templates, and commissions major photography/illustration.
    3. *Senior Designer*: Executes complex feature spreads, cover designs, and template adaptations.
    4. *Junior Designer / Intern*: Handles page layout, image resizing, color corrections, sidebars, and digital asset formatting.
    5. *Picture Editor / Photo Editor*: Commissions photographers, sources stock imagery, handles image licensing and model releases.
    6. *Sub-Editor / Copy Editor*: Writes headlines, stand-firsts, captions, and verifies text fitting and proofreading.

## Key Concepts
- **Editorial Design**: The visual communication and arrangement of text and images in publications (magazines, newspapers, books, digital editions) to create a clear reading structure and distinct brand voice.
- **Multi-Platform Publishing**: Producing editorial content simultaneously for print, web, tablet, and social channels using unified visual identity systems.
- **Page Furniture**: Functional typographic and graphic elements (folios, running headers, section markers, categories) that guide navigation across pages.
- **Stand-First (Deck)**: A brief introductory paragraph positioned between the main headline and body copy that expands on the headline and hooks the reader.
- **Flatplan**: A visual diagram or map showing the page layout sequence of an entire issue, tracking advertising placements, feature locations, and color sections.
- **Commissioning**: Briefing external creative talent (photographers, illustrators, stylists) to produce unique visual assets for a publication.

## Mental Models
- **Think of Editorial Design as a Director's Cut**: The designer does not merely "decorate" text; they act as a stage director, deciding when the audience reads silently, when a image creates drama, and when a pull quote stops them in their tracks.
- **Use "Anchor and Flow" Across Platforms**: Anchor the core editorial brand (masthead, primary typefaces, tone of voice) across all outputs, but allow the layout flow to change dynamically based on the medium's interaction model (tactile flipping vs. touch swiping vs. vertical scrolling).

## Anti-patterns
- **The PDF Mirror**: Simply uploading raw print PDFs onto a website or tablet app without re-optimizing typography, line lengths, or interactive navigation.
- **Decorating Without Reading**: Designing a layout without reading the text first. Layout choices must reinforce the article's emotional tone and narrative structure.
- **Feature Clutter**: Applying heavy graphic embellishments to every page, creating visual fatigue and eliminating contrast between news pages and feature spreads.

## Worked Example

### Case Study Breakdown: *Wallpaper\** Multi-Platform Workflow
When *Wallpaper\** produces a major feature, the art department executes a coordinated multi-platform design strategy:

1. **Print Edition (Double-Page Spread)**:
   - Uses a generous 6-column grid with wide margins.
   - High-impact, full-bleed photography on coated stock.
   - Serif body text with precise baseline grid alignment.
2. **Tablet / iPad Edition**:
   - Re-layouts the 6-column print spread into a dual-orientation system (portrait single-column vs. landscape 2-column).
   - Embedded video clip from the photo shoot plays automatically on swipe.
   - Interactive hotspots allow readers to tap furniture items to view price, designer details, and stockist links.
3. **Web / Mobile Edition**:
   - Collapses into a single responsive column optimized for vertical scrolling.
   - High-contrast sans-serif typography tuned for screen readability.
   - Compressed WebP image galleries with lazy-loading for fast page loads.

## Key Takeaways
1. Editorial design is a collaborative discipline that combines editorial strategy, typography, photography, and interaction design.
2. Successful publications establish a strong brand identity that remains recognizable whether experienced on newsstand paper or a mobile screen.
3. Designers must understand the distinct mechanics of print (tactile pacing, fixed spreads) vs. digital (responsive flows, motion, interactivity).
4. Clear division of labor between editors, art directors, sub-editors, and picture editors ensures high visual and journalistic standards.
5. Student and junior portfolios should demonstrate strong attention to detail: error-free proofreading, correct line measure, and multi-platform adaptations.
