# Glossary of Editorial Design Terms

A complete alphabetical dictionary of editorial typography, layout, print production, and digital publishing terminology extracted from *Editorial Design: Digital and Print* by Cath Caldwell and Yolanda Zappaterra.

---

### A
- **Abstract Cover** — A cover design strategy using symbolic illustration, 3D graphics, or conceptual metaphors rather than realistic portrait photography (Ch 3).
- **Anchor Point** — A prominent visual element (such as an oversized headline or striking photo) that serves as the reader's first entry point into a layout (Ch 5).
- **Asymmetric Balance** — Visual equilibrium achieved by balancing elements of unequal size, color, or visual weight across a spread (Ch 5).

### B
- **Back-of-Book (BOB)** — The rear section of a publication containing structured listings, reviews, directories, index, and columnists (Ch 4).
- **Baseline Grid** — A series of invisible equidistant horizontal lines set to the body copy leading, to which text baselines and image frames snap (Ch 6).
- **Berliner (Midi)** — A newspaper format measuring approximately 315 × 470 mm, popular in European press (Ch 2).
- **Bleed** — Printing artwork or photography beyond the page trim edge so that image color runs seamlessly to the physical page border after trimming (Ch 6, Ch 7).
- **Body Copy** — The main continuous reading text of an article, set in a legible size and leading (Ch 4, Ch 6).
- **Broadsheet** — The largest traditional newspaper format (approx. 375 × 600 mm), characterized by dense multi-column grids (Ch 2).
- **Byline** — The typographic credit line giving the name of the article's author or photographer (Ch 4).

### C
- **Caption** — Concise explanatory text paired directly with an image, illustration, or graphic (Ch 4).
- **CMYK** — Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key (Black); the subtractive 4-color printing process model used for physical ink production (Ch 6).
- **Column Gutter** — The narrow vertical margin or channel separating adjacent text columns (Ch 6).
- **Commissioning** — Briefing and hiring external creative talent (photographers, illustrators) to produce original artwork for a publication (Ch 1).
- **Contents Page (ToC)** — A navigational spread at the front of a publication listing major features, section pages, and editor picks (Ch 4).
- **Cover Line** — A headline printed on the cover highlighting key features or articles inside to attract newsstand buyers (Ch 3).

### D
- **Deck (Stand-First)** — A brief introductory paragraph set between the headline and body copy that expands on the title and hooks the reader (Ch 4).
- **Double-Page Spread (DPS)** — Two facing pages in a bound publication designed and viewed together as a single visual canvas (Ch 5).
- **Drop Cap (Initial Cap)** — An oversized capital letter at the start of an article dropping down 2 to 5 lines of body text to mark the reading entry point (Ch 5).

### F
- **Feature Well** — The middle section of a publication containing long-form stories and major photo essays with high layout freedom (Ch 4).
- **Finite Publishing** — Delivering digital content in distinct, curated "issues" or packages with a clear start and end point (Ch 7).
- **Flatplan** — A master visual diagram mapping the exact page sequence of an issue, tracking editorial content, ad placements, and signatures (Ch 4, Ch 6).
- **Flowline (Hang Line)** — An invisible horizontal alignment guide across columns that anchors image tops or headline start points across spreads (Ch 5).
- **Folio** — Functional navigational page furniture consisting of page numbers, publication name, section header, and date in page margins (Ch 4).
- **Front-of-Book (FOB)** — The opening section of a publication featuring news bites, trends, opinion pieces, and short items (Ch 4).

### G
- **Grid** — A structural matrix of columns, gutters, margins, and flowlines used to align and organize content on a page or screen (Ch 6).
- **Gutenberg Diagram** — A reading pattern model describing how the eye sweeps across a printed spread in a Z-pattern from top-left to bottom-right (Ch 5).

### H
- **Headline** — The largest typographic element on an article spread, functioning as the primary verbal and visual hook (Ch 4).
- **House Style** — A standardized set of design, typographic, editorial, and photographic guidelines enforcing publication brand consistency (Ch 6).

### I
- **Infographic** — A visual representation of data, statistics, maps, or timelines using diagrams, charts, and custom iconography (Ch 6).

### L
- **Leading** — The vertical distance measured in points from the baseline of one line of text to the baseline of the next (Ch 6).
- **Liquid Layout** — A responsive digital layout system where page elements dynamically reflow and scale to fit any viewport dimension (Ch 2).

### M
- **Masthead** — The designed title logo of a publication prominently displayed at the top of the cover (Ch 3).
- **Margin** — Inactive buffer space surrounding the live grid area along top, bottom, inside, and outside page borders (Ch 6).

### N
- **Newsstand Cover** — A cover version designed for retail shelves, featuring high visual contrast, bold colors, and multiple cover lines (Ch 3).

### O
- **Orphan** — A single line of a paragraph left alone at the top of a column or page, isolated from the rest of the text block (Ch 6).

### P
- **Page Furniture** — Functional graphic and typographic elements (folios, headers, section tags, captions) that navigate the reader (Ch 4).
- **Paywall** — A digital access barrier requiring a paid subscription or registration to read online editorial content (Ch 2).
- **Pull Quote** — An extracted sentence or provocative quote set in large type to break up body copy and re-engage casual readers (Ch 4).

### R
- **RGB** — Red, Green, Blue; the additive color model used for screen displays and digital device rendering (Ch 6).

### S
- **Spine Gutter** — The inner margin where facing pages meet at the binding; critical copy and face details must remain clear of this area (Ch 5).
- **Stand-First** — See *Deck* (Ch 4).
- **Subhead (Cross-head)** — Short bold titles inserted between body text paragraphs to break copy density and aid scanning (Ch 4).
- **Subscriber Cover** — A cover version created exclusively for direct subscribers, featuring minimal text and pristine full-bleed artwork (Ch 3).

### T
- **Tabloid (Compact)** — A smaller newspaper format (approx. 280 × 430 mm) utilizing bold headlines and 4–5 column grids (Ch 2).

### W
- **White Space (Negative Space)** — Empty, unprinted areas surrounding layout elements, essential for visual focus and breathing room (Ch 5).
- **Widow** — A short single word left alone on the last line of a paragraph or column (Ch 6).

### Z
- **Zine** — A small-run, self-published print publication focused on niche subcultures, art, or activist themes (Ch 2).
