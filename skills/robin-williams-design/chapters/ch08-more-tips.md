# Chapter 8: 更多提示与技巧 (More Tips & Techniques)

## Core Idea
A practical application chapter: takes the 4 principles and applies them across 9 common real-world projects — branding/packaging, business cards, letterhead & envelopes, flyers, newsletters, brochures, postcards, newspaper ads, and resumes.

## Frameworks Introduced

### 1. Creating Packaging/Branding (创建包装或品牌)
- **Repetition is the key**: Every piece in a brand family must share a signature image or style. Deliberate repetition → obvious branding. Find the similarity that becomes your "signature move."
- Example: The Shakespeare Papers bimonthly — each issue is fully redesigned but linked by a consistent colored band on the left and consistent type placement. The brand extends to spin-off products (program notes, readers' editions) that share the identity but differ clearly.

### 2. Business Cards (企业名片)
- **Remove all non-essential info**: a card is not a brochure. Drop the words "phone/email/website" — the formats make them obvious.
- **Choose horizontal OR vertical**: often vertical suits the dense info better — try both.
- **Font size is the #1 beginner problem**: 10–11pt looks huge on a card; 12pt looks clunky. 8–9pt is typical for professional cards. Collect 3 cards you admire — they won't use 12pt. The card holds info the client needs in seconds; overall refinement beats max legibility.
- **Design card + letterhead + envelope together** as one consistent image.

### 3. Letterhead & Envelopes (信笺和信封)
- Design with the card; they should reinforce each other.
- **Standard envelope**: #10 = 9½ × 4 in (US); C4 = 110 × 220 mm (Europe).
- **Create a focal point**: one dominant element present on card, letterhead, AND envelope.
- **One alignment**: don't center the top and left-align the rest. Be bold: right-align along the edge with wide line spacing, huge company name on top, or a large faded logo as a watermark behind the text area.
- **Second page**: reuse one small element from page 1 on page 2 (e.g., print 800 first pages / 200 second pages).
- **Fax/copy caveat**: avoid dark or dotted papers, large dark areas, reverse text, tiny fonts (lost in fax/copy). If faxing a lot, design a dedicated fax version.

### 4. Flyers (传单)
- The place to be brave! Flyers compete with dozens of others on a bulletin board — being unusual achieves 90% of the goal.
- **The #1 problem is missing contrast + no hierarchy**: if everything is big, nothing stands out. Create ONE focal point and use contrast to guide the eye.
- **Contrast-y subheads**: strong visual subheads let readers scan quickly; without them they'll throw the flyer away.
- **Repetition**: reuse the headline's distinctive font somewhere in the body (one letter, a subhead, a drop cap, bullets).
- **One alignment**, bold and clear.
- Context matters: mailed flyer can carry more content; a kiosk flyer must be readable at a glance.
- Try: images at 2× planned size, a 400pt headline instead of 24pt, or a minimalist flyer with one line of 10pt text.

### 5. Newsletters (新闻简报)
- Biggest problems: lack of alignment, lack of contrast, too much Arial/Helvetica or Times New Roman.
- **Alignment**: one alignment, consistently; rules should start/end aligned with other elements (column edges); trim photos that overflow columns by ¼ inch; when breaking alignment, break it fully (a slight offset reads as a mistake).
- **Indentation**: first paragraph never indented; use 1 em standard indent (≈2 spaces, not 5); paragraphs get EITHER extra space OR indent, never both.
- **Don't use Helvetica/Arial**: too weak for contrast. Use a sans family with both a heavy black and a light version (Eurostile, Formata, Syntax, Frutiger, Myriad) for headlines; or a decorative face; or color.
- **Readable body**: classic old-style serif (Garamond, Jenson, Caslon, Minion, Palatino) or a light slab serif (Clarendon, Bookman, Kepler, New Century Schoolbook); if sans, wide leading and short line length.
- Whitespace is fine but must be organized — never trapped between elements.
- The masthead sets the tone for the whole newsletter.

### 6. Brochures (宣传册)
- Fold a paper sample BEFORE designing; mark each panel; read order = the order a reader opens it (never put copyright/contact on the first panel).
- **Front and back panel widths differ** — fold the dummy and measure; don't just divide 11" into 3.
- Avoid important info in the fold/crease — but with clear alignment lines, images can straddle gutters/folds.
- Apply the 4 principles: contrast (hierarchy), repetition (unify), alignment (strong sharp edges; deliberate breaks only with contrasting alignment elsewhere), proximity (space reflects relationship; use paragraph before/after spacing, NOT double Enter — double Enter creates too-big gaps and equal space above/below headings).

### 7. Postcards (明信片)
- Be **different** (oversized/odd shapes — check postal rules), **serial** (a series makes more impression than one), **specific** (tell them the benefit and the price), **concise** (one short message on the front; details on back), **colorful** (colors attract).
- **Determine the key**: what impression do you want? Luxury product → look expensive; bargain → don't look too fine (Saks vs. Kmart — every look has a purpose and a market).
- Contrast is the postcard's best friend (title vs. body, ink vs. ink, ink vs. paper — and blank space as contrast).
- Same rules as business cards: no corner-stuffing, no filling every blank, no all-elements-same-size.

### 8. Newspaper Ads (报纸广告)
- **The biggest problem is overcrowding** — clients think they must fill every paid inch.
- **Use blank space for contrast**: on a crowded page, whitespace draws the eye irresistibly. With whitespace, the headline can be an elegant script or old-style face instead of a huge blocky font.
- **Font choice on porous paper**: ink spreads; avoid delicate small serifs and thin rules unless set large enough.
- **Reverse text (white on dark)**: avoid on cheap paper if possible; if used, pick a font without thin strokes (ink fills them), and set it slightly larger/bolder (reverse text looks smaller/thinner).
- Ad copy: blank, clever headline, clear call-to-action (with route: phone/email/web), concise.

### 9. Resumes (简历)
- Visual tips only — content is your domain. How bold you can be depends on the field (graphic designers can be creative; other fields are conservative).
- **Contrast**: make important elements stand out — even subtle-but-clear contrast works on conservative pages.
- **Repetition**: consistent structure — same date placement everywhere, repeat alignment, repeat any accent color (lines, bullets).
- **Alignment**: crucial for a clean, professional whole.
- **Proximity**: keep headings close to their related info; pull bullets close to their items.
- **Match the medium**: mailed, online, or online-but-printed versions need different layouts, type, page size, color.

## Key Takeaways
1. Branding = disciplined repetition of a signature element across all pieces.
2. Cards/postcards: cut non-essentials, smaller type (8–9pt on cards), one focal point, never fill every blank.
3. Flyers: one focal point + contrast-y subheads + one alignment; be brave.
4. Newsletters/brochures: one alignment consistently; 1em indents; no Helvetica/Arial; classic serif body; space via paragraph settings, not double Enter.
5. Newspaper ads: whitespace is the contrast weapon; mind ink spread; beware reverse text.
6. Brochures: fold a dummy, mind panel widths and the fold.
7. Resumes: contrast + consistent repetition + alignment + proximity; match the medium.
