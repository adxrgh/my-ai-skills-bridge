# Chapter 9: 字体的基本规则 (Basic Type Rules)

## Core Idea
Many "amateur" typing habits come from typewriter-era constraints that no longer apply. This chapter is a checklist of professional typography rules that instantly separate amateur work from professional work — one space after punctuation, real quotes/apostrophes, proper dashes, special characters, no ALL-CAPS-for-emphasis, no underlining, kerning, and avoiding widows/orphans.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Typography hygiene rules (the checklist):
1. **One space after punctuation**: NEVER two. Proportional type makes two spaces look like gaping holes. Whatever the historical tradition, today's standard is one space. Books and magazines never use two.
2. **Use real (curly) quotes, not typewriter quotes**: Opening quotes look like 66; closing like 99. Typewriter quotes are the #1 glaring amateur sign. Software often inserts correct ones — check. In US English, commas/periods go INSIDE quotes (always); colons/semicolons OUTSIDE; question/exclamation marks inside only if part of the quote. Multi-paragraph quotes: opening quote at start of EVERY paragraph, closing quote only at the end of the last.
3. **Apostrophes**: The apostrophe is a single closing quote (the "9" shape). Never use the typewriter/foot mark. Common errors: (1) typewriter shape, (2) wrong placement. It marks possession or omitted letters (Rock 'n' Roll = Rock AND Roll — the apostrophe is a 9-shape, not an "n").
4. **Hyphens vs. en dashes vs. em dashes**:
   - **Hyphen** (-): joins words (daughter-in-law) or breaks lines at word wrap.
   - **En dash** (–, Option+- on Mac): a duration/range (October–December, 7:30–9:30 p.m., 7–12 years) — reads as "to"; also in compound adjectives like San Francisco–Chicago flight. NO spaces around it.
   - **Em dash** (—): 2× the en dash; signals a sudden thought change or a break stronger than a comma but weaker than a period. NO spaces; optional narrow spaces if your software supports tracking (never full spaces).
   - Never use two hyphens (--) — that's a typewriter-era workaround.
5. **Special characters**: OpenType fonts contain ~16,000 glyphs. Use the Glyphs panel (InDesign) or Character Map; keyboard shortcuts (Mac: Option/Shift combos; Windows: Alt+ANSI codes) for © ™ ¢ ° … • etc. (full tables included in the chapter).
6. **Accent marks**: Mac — hold Option + dead key, then the letter (résumé: Option+e then e). Windows — ANSI codes.

### Typography aesthetics rules:
7. **Don't use ALL CAPS for emphasis**: We read words by their SHAPE — especially the "coastline" of the upper half of the word. ALL CAPS forces letter-by-letter reading — slower and tiring. Use ALL CAPS only when you can state a real reason (e.g., "I need the logo text to be a rectangle"). Don't assume bigger = more readable.
8. **Never underline**: Underlining was a typewriter signal to the typesetter meaning "set in italic." You ARE the typesetter now — use italics. For emphasis use bold, larger size, different font, color, or combinations. If you must use a line, customize its height/length/position — never the default underline.
9. **Kerning (字距调整)**: Moving character spacing for visual evenness. Word processors can't do it well — that's how pros spot word-processed text. The larger the type, the more important. It's a purely visual, eye-driven task (WA, LO pairs need attention; round letters look smaller than square ones of equal size). Learn manual kerning in your software.
10. **Widows & orphans**: A widow = last line of a paragraph with <7 characters (or worse: a hyphenated fragment). An orphan = a paragraph's final short line stranded at the top of the next column/page. Never allow either — fix by rewriting, adjusting letter/word/line spacing, or tiny page-margin tweaks.

### Fine-point rules ("Other"):
- **Punctuation following styled text**: punctuation right after bold/italic/different-font text takes the same style (the colon after a bold word is bold too).
- **Punctuation inside/outside parentheses**: if the parenthetical is part of a sentence, punctuation goes OUTSIDE the closing paren; if the parenthetical is a complete sentence, punctuation goes INSIDE.
- **Paragraph indent**: 1 em (≈2 spaces) — NOT 5 spaces or a half-inch Tab default. 1 em = the point size of your type (12pt type → 12pt indent).
- **Indent OR paragraph spacing — never both**: both mean "new paragraph"; choose one system.
- **First paragraph after a title/subhead is never indented.**
- **Text in boxes**: leave generous, even space on all sides.
- **Lists use bullets or decorative glyphs, not hyphens** — hyphens in lists look terrible; use a real bullet (Mac Option+8, Windows Alt 0149) or ornaments.

## Key Concepts
- **The typewriter legacy**: monospaced fonts, no italics (used apostrophes), no real dashes/symbols, no bold (used ALL CAPS/underline) — all obsolete, but habits persist.
- **Reading by word shape**: the "coastline" of a word's upper half drives recognition — ALL CAPS destroys it.
- **Kerning is visual, not mathematical**: characters leave different visual footprints; space must LOOK even (the circle-vs-square illusion).

## Mental Models
- **"You are the typesetter now"**: every rule here assumes you've replaced the typewriter/proofreader pipeline — act accordingly.
- **"Amateur tells are instantly visible to the trained eye"**: quotes, double spaces, default underlines, hyphen dashes — trained readers spot them immediately; they erode credibility (e.g., a million-dollar ad with a typewriter apostrophe).
- **"Have a reason you can state"**: for ALL CAPS and other rule-breaking — if you can't say the reason, don't do it.

## Worked Example
**Typing "résumé" on a Mac**: type r → hold Option+e (see a highlighted space/nothing) → release → type e → é appears; repeat for the final é.

## Key Takeaways
1. One space after punctuation — always.
2. Curly quotes and 9-shaped apostrophes; correct placement.
3. Hyphen vs. en dash (ranges) vs. em dash (breaks) — no double hyphens.
4. ALL CAPS and underlines are amateur emphasis — use shape-based reading, italics, bold, size, color.
5. Kern large type manually; it's visual, not formulaic.
6. No widows/orphans — fix them at all costs.
7. 1 em indents, indent-OR-space, no first-paragraph indent, styled punctuation, bullets not hyphens.
