# Chapter 14: 测验答案和建议 (Quiz Answers & Suggestions)

## Core Idea
Answers to all nine quizzes plus "designer's eye" suggestions for every before/after example in the book. The chapter models a teaching philosophy: writing down the *correct* information helps you remember it — so check answers against your own, discuss with friends, and apply the principles to every page around you.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The teaching philosophy**: all quizzes are open-book and open-discussion. Getting the answer RIGHT during the test (rather than guessing wrong and forgetting) is the learning moment.
- **"Open your eyes"**: improving visual acuity (视觉敏锐度) is the key to appreciation and skill.

## Key Concepts
### Quiz answers (condensed):
1. **Design principles quiz**: typical resume fixes — remove borders (let it breathe), proximity (title near its content, remove double returns, add space above headings, separate personal info), alignment (single clear left alignment; hanging indents for wrapped lines), repetition (stronger bullets, bolder headings), contrast (bold headings, strong bullets; optional: old-style figures or smaller numerals).
2. **Color quiz**: analogous = adjacent colors; complementary = opposite colors; tint = hue + white; shade = hue + black; print = CMYK; screen = RGB.
3. **Apostrophe quiz**: it's = "it is" (always); its = possessive. Nested apostrophes: Mom 'n' Pop, fishin', '60s, cookies 'n' cream.
4. **Type categories**: Oldstyle / Modern / Slab / Sans / Script / Decorative matched to sample words.
5. **Thick/thin transition**: moderate (Oldstyle), severe (Modern), none (Slab/Sans).
6. **Serifs**: thin horizontal (Modern), thick slab (Slab), none (Sans), slanted (Oldstyle).
7. **Contrast vs. conflict** (Quiz #8):
   - *Fancy Perfume* = **conflict** — too many similarities (all caps, same size, similar fancy fonts and weights).
   - *Dogfood* = **contrast** — strong contrasts in size, color, shape (caps vs. lowercase, roman vs. italic), weight, structure.
   - *My Mother* = **conflict** — same size, similar weight, same structure, same roman shape.
   - *Funny Farm* = **conflict** — has contrast potential but differences too weak; worse, two focal points compete (a larger thin font vs. a smaller bold caps font). Decide which is dominant and emphasize only that.
   - *Let's Dance* = **contrast** — same family (Formata) but strong weight, shape (roman/italic, caps/lowercase), structure, and color contrast.
8. **Dos and Don'ts** (Quiz #9):
   - Don't: two scripts together; two fonts from the same category; making one element bolder while another is bigger (they compete — pick one to emphasize); script + italic; two "interesting" fonts (they compete for attention); interesting-but-unreadable layouts.
   - Do: tall-thin + short-wide pairing; strong thick/thin + sans/slab pairing; always remember the 4 basic principles; breaking rules is fine once you know the rules.

### Designer's-eye suggestions (patterns distilled from many examples):
- **Focal point & scale**: make the title/headline bigger; make secondary text genuinely smaller; enlarge images; let a large element (pie, moon, heart) float/overlap for interest.
- **Proximity**: pull text close to related numbers/images; give photos and contact info breathing room; separate unrelated groups.
- **Alignment**: establish ONE clear alignment; align title with photo edge, bullets with text; use 1 em indents; remove default indents after headings; avoid wrapping text that breaks alignment.
- **Repetition**: match headline font to the logo/title font; repeat colors, weights, rules, spacing; use consistent bullets; repeat the logo's color in details (e.g., red dashed rule).
- **Contrast & style**: remove borders (or thin them to 0.5pt); change ALL-CAPS to mixed case (bigger type possible); swap Times/Helvetica for more distinctive faces; use reverse text on dark bands; avoid two competing black blocks; avoid two logos competing.
- **Cleanup**: delete unnecessary words (phone/email/zip for local ads, "available", extra URLs); spell out abbreviations (Appts → Appointments); capitalize URLs for readability; replace commas with bullets; remove random clip art.

## Mental Models
- **"The correct answer is the memorable one"**: learning by writing down right answers beats guessing.
- **"Every example is a lesson"**: the before/after pairs throughout the book are the real curriculum — study each fix.

## Anti-patterns (from the answers)
- Two focal points competing (Funny Farm).
- Two "interesting" fonts or two scripts/italics together.
- Same-size/same-weight/same-structure fonts (My Mother).
- Borders everywhere, ALL-CAPS everywhere, weak fonts (Times/Helvetica), commas instead of bullets, unreadable URLs.

## Key Takeaways
1. The answers reinforce: name the problem (similarity), then fix with contrast.
2. In every redesign, all four principles appear — the fixes are rarely single-principle.
3. Small details matter: borders, ALL-CAPS, URLs, abbreviations, bullet characters.
4. Quiz 8/9 are the book's final exam: distinguish contrast from conflict by hunting similarities.
