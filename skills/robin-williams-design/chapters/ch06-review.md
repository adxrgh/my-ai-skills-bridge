# Chapter 6: 4大基本原则复习 (Review of the 4 Basic Principles)

## Core Idea
A synthesis chapter: applies all four principles — Proximity, Alignment, Repetition, Contrast — to a single dull report cover, and frames the general rule of design (and life): **don't be a wimp**. Includes two self-tests for applying the principles.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The "Don't Be a Wimp" Life/Design Guideline**:
  - Don't be afraid of blank space — it lets the eye (and mind) rest.
  - Don't be afraid of asymmetric, non-centered layouts — off-center often hits harder.
  - Don't be afraid of very large or very small words — appropriate volume is fine.
  - Don't be afraid of very large or very small images if the result supports the design.
- **The 4-principle application system** (a repeatable workflow on any piece):
  1. **Proximity**: group related items (title + subtitle as one unit; author/date farther away as separate-but-related info).
  2. **Alignment**: align every element to a boundary; left/right alignment gives a stronger, more refined edge than centered.
  3. **Repetition**: pick an existing repeated element and make it prominent; telegraph connections across physical distance (e.g., the title's distinctive font repeated in the author's name; dashed lines reused).
  4. **Contrast**: add strong black/white contrast (e.g., a black box), use color (deep red) that serves double duty as contrast and repetition, play bold vs. thin type and ALL-CAPS vs. mixed case.

## Key Concepts
- **Centered vs. aligned**: The report cover was previously "aligned" but centered — that's weak. Left/right alignment provides a clear baseline for the eye and a more refined look.
- **Repetition bridges distance**: The title's unusual font repeated in the author's name connects elements that are physically far apart on the page.
- **Contrast done right**: Pure black/white; bold vs. thin weight; ALL-CAPS vs. lowercase — all strong, never wishy-washy.

## Mental Models
- **"Don't be a wimp"** — the book's central philosophy; apply to blank space, asymmetry, scale, and contrast.
- **Title+subtitle = one unit**: Header information grouping is the first move on any cover or document.
- **Borrowing is professional**: Professional designers constantly "steal" ideas — find a flyer/business card/newsletter you love and adapt its layout with your own text and images until it becomes yours. All designers do this.

## Worked Example
**The dull report cover, progressively improved**:
1. **Starting point**: Times New Roman, centered, rows equally spaced — reads as 6 separate unrelated topics.
2. **+ Proximity**: Title+subtitle pulled together into one unit; author/date pushed farther away.
3. **+ Alignment**: Switch centered → left/right alignment for a strong, refined edge.
4. **+ Repetition**: Title's distinctive font repeated in the author's name; dashed line introduced as a reusable motif.
5. **+ Contrast**: Add a black box / deep red color / bold vs. thin type / ALL-CAPS vs. mixed — the page now grabs the eye.

**Quiz #2 — Magazine ad redesign workflow**:
- Remove all extraneous stuff first: drop `http://`, "website"/"email" words, unnecessary boxes, ALL-CAPS, round borders (look "weak" and clash with sharp logo corners). Thin out or remove borders.
- Editorially tighten the text.
- Determine the focal point (depends on where the ad runs), then structure the other information around it.
- Fix proximity grouping, pick 1–2 fonts, align, add a repeated element, and create strong contrast.
- Proposed example: "Moonstone Dreamcatchers" ad before/after + an alternate logo treatment.

## Key Takeaways
1. All four principles work as a system — rarely apply only one.
2. "Don't be a wimp" is the master guideline: size, space, asymmetry, contrast should be decisive.
3. A strong single workflow: phrase the problem → group (proximity) → align → repeat → contrast.
4. Professional designers borrow layouts and adapt them — start from templates you admire.
5. Don't be too serious about design — follow the four principles and have fun; results follow.
