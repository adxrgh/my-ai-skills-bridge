# Chapter 13: 你掌握了吗 (Did You Get It?)

## Core Idea
A practical synthesis: with the right typeface families and a repeatable 5-step design process, you can confidently design any page. This chapter gives the process, a training exercise (redesign a bad ad with tracing paper), and a worked example.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 5-Step Design Process**:
  1. **Start with the focal point (中心点)**: decide what the reader should see FIRST. Unless you deliberately want a harmonious design, create a strong-contrast focal point.
  2. **Group the information (分组/亲密性)**: group logically; show relationships through physical closeness.
  3. **Establish and maintain clear alignment (对齐)**: if there's an obvious edge (photo edge, vertical rule), reinforce it by aligning other elements to it.
  4. **Create or amplify repetition (重复)**: use bold type, rules, ornaments, or spatial layout; find what already repeats and push it further.
  5. **Create strong contrast (对比)**: black-and-white clarity. If EVERYTHING is big/bold/fancy, there is NO contrast. Contrast through bigger/bolder OR smaller/thinner — the key is clear difference that draws the eye.
- **The "You're done when you can't improve it" mindset**: if you only scratch the surface of the redesign, the result won't be much better — the better you do, the more dramatic the result.

## Key Concepts
- **You only need a few type families**: from each category pick one family; make sure your sans family includes very light AND very heavy weights. With these, you can create endless lively combinations.
- **Print it before judging**: screen-only designs often disappoint on paper (paper + ink). Show clients paper versions of anything that will be printed.

## Mental Models
- **"Find the problem before the solution"**: use your trained eye to spot bad design in newspapers/magazines/web — that IS the exercise.
- **"Make the center the center"**: if multiple elements compete to be the focal point, none is; you must choose one and subordinate the rest.
- **The client trick (from the classroom)**: when a client insists on their own poor design, present their version *deliberately crumpled* (coffee stain, ragged edges, pen marks, crooked placement) and yours cleanly on excellent paper, mounted and protected. The "untrained eye" (VIP) sees your version as clearly better. (Author's playful advice.)

## Worked Example
**The redesign exercise**: trace a bad ad from a magazine/newspaper/web page. Move the tracing paper and redraw parts: provide clear alignment lines, tighten proximity, make the focal point genuinely central, change ALL-CAPS to lowercase, make some items bolder/smaller/larger, remove obvious junk. Repeat until it's dramatically better.

**The little poster**: not terrible but needs help — main problems are lack of clear alignment and multiple competing focal points. Redraw the elements with tracing paper to fix.

## Key Takeaways
1. Five-step process: focal point → group → align → repeat → strong contrast.
2. A strong focal point is created by contrast; everything else supports it.
3. Choose a few good type families (one per category, sans with full weight range) and master them.
4. Practice redesigning bad ads with tracing paper — the more thorough, the better the result.
5. Print out designs destined for paper — screen ≠ print.
