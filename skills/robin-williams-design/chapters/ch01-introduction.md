# Chapter 1: 引言 (Introduction)

## Core Idea
The four basic design principles — Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, and Proximity (CRAP) — are simpler than they appear. Once you can name and recognize them, you will see them everywhere and be able to control your designs deliberately.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Joshua Tree Principle**: Once you can name something, you will notice it everywhere. Naming a design problem is the first step to mastering it.
- **The 1-2-3 Method for Learning Design**:
  1. **Learn** the 4 basic principles — they are simpler than you think.
  2. **Recognize** when you are not using them — state the problem in words.
  3. **Apply** the principles — the results will surprise you.
- **CRAP Framework** (the 4 basic principles):
  - **Contrast (对比)**: Avoid similar elements. If they are not the same, make them very different. Contrast is usually the most important factor for attracting the reader's eye.
  - **Repetition (重复)**: Repeat visual elements throughout the work — colors, shapes, textures, spatial relationships, line weights, fonts, sizes, images. This adds order and strengthens unity.
  - **Alignment (对齐)**: Nothing should be placed arbitrarily. Every element should have a visual connection to another element on the page. This creates a clean, crisp appearance.
  - **Proximity (亲密性)**: Related items should be grouped together physically. If items have a close relationship, they become a visual unit rather than isolated elements, reducing clutter and providing structure.

## Key Concepts
- **Crystallized expertise**: Books contain frameworks that took years to develop; naming them gives you control.
- **Visual connection**: Even physically separated elements can feel connected through alignment.
- **Visual unit**: Multiple items perceived as a single group due to spatial proximity.

## Mental Models
- **"Name it to tame it"**: Just as knowing the name "Joshua tree" made the author see them everywhere, naming design flaws lets you control them.
- **Think of design as problem-solving, not decoration**: The 4 principles are tools for organizing and clarifying information, not merely making things pretty.
- **Principles are interconnected**: Though discussed individually, the 4 principles almost always work together in real designs.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring the principles because they seem too simple**: The principles are simple, but their power comes from deliberate, conscious application.
- **Applying only one principle**: Good design usually requires all four principles working in concert.

## Key Takeaways
1. The 4 principles (CRAP) are the foundation of every good design.
2. The first step to mastery is being able to name and identify the principles in action (or in violation).
3. Contrast is usually the strongest tool for attracting attention.
4. Proximity creates visual units and organizes information before the reader even reads the words.
5. Alignment creates invisible connections between elements.
6. Repetition provides unity and continuity across pages.
