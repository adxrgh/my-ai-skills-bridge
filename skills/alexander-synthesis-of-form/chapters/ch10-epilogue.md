# Epilogue: The Form of the Process

## Core Idea
Every component is simultaneously a unit within a larger pattern and a pattern organizing smaller units. A mature design process creates a physical hierarchy corresponding to the problem's functional hierarchy and develops judgment by making the path to form explicit.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Pattern-unit duality**
  - When to use: designing components, modules, services, or organizational units across scales.
  - How: for every component ask what larger whole it serves as a unit, and what smaller components it organizes as a pattern.
- **Process as an object of design**
  - Formalize the path by which results are produced so it can be criticized, taught, and improved.
- **Structural correspondence**
  - Use functional decomposition to discover physical components; do not assume the correspondence must be visually literal.

## Key Concepts
- **Component**: both a bounded entity and an arrangement of subordinate entities.
- **Organization**: the relational quality that makes a shape a form.
- **Process form**: the explicit organization of the path that produces a result.
- **Structural correspondence**: similarity between the problem's pattern and the synthesis process or artifact hierarchy.

## Mental Models
- Zoom in: every unit becomes a pattern. Zoom out: every pattern becomes a unit.
- Better formal methods initially improve accuracy, but their deeper value is enabling new orders of thought.
- The point of representing design is not only a better answer today but a more developable discipline tomorrow.

## Anti-patterns
- **Component as isolated piece**: ignores the pattern it creates and the larger pattern it joins.
- **Result-only evaluation**: discards knowledge about how the form was reached.
- **Method resistance as defense of genius**: protects status while preventing collective improvement.

## Worked Example
Alexander's imaginary society estimates field area by gifted intuition and resists arithmetic. Formal arithmetic first appears merely to improve accuracy, but its larger consequence is that operations themselves become objects of thought, enabling higher mathematics. Likewise, explicit design processes may initially aid difficult briefs, yet their deeper promise is a more refined feeling for form.

## Key Takeaways
1. Design every component at two scales: as unit and as pattern.
2. Preserve the reasoning path so the discipline can learn from it.
3. Formalization should enlarge, not suppress, physical intuition.
4. Seek organizational correspondence between problem and form.
