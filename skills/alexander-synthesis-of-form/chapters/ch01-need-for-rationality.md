# Chapter 1: The Need for Rationality

## Core Idea
Modern design problems contain more interacting requirements than unaided intuition can reliably integrate. Analysis should externalize the problem's organization so intuition can work on smaller, coherent parts; it should not prescribe form.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Problem representation as an aid to synthesis**
  - When to use: the relevant facts and interactions exceed one person's simultaneous grasp.
  - How: make requirements and relations explicit; manipulate that representation; return to physical judgment for synthesis.
  - Why it works: writing changes a cognitively impossible whole into inspectable subproblems, as notation does for arithmetic.
- **Logic as a picture, not a dictator**
  - Use formal structures to expose assumptions and relations. Do not claim that deduction alone determines a physical form.
- **Loss of intellectual innocence**
  - Once intuitive choices can be represented and criticized, neither intuition nor method may remain exempt from reasons.

## Key Concepts
- **Synthesis**: inventing physical order in response to function.
- **Interaction**: a relation in which satisfying one requirement changes the prospects of satisfying another.
- **Arbitrary formal order**: a simple visual idea imposed because the actual problem is not understood.
- **Representation**: an explicit, manipulable picture of a problem's structure.
- **Personal bias**: unavoidable judgment whose point of entry should be made visible and controlled.

## Mental Models
- Treat a complex brief like long division: if it cannot be held in the head, improve the notation before demanding more intuition.
- Use analysis to organize the question and intuition to create form; neither replaces the other.
- Prefer a criticizable explicit picture to an impressive but uninspectable rationale.

## Anti-patterns
- **The fetish of intuition**: protects decisions from criticism and fails when complexity exceeds cognitive capacity.
- **Style as borrowed certainty**: replaces unresolved forces with a known visual language.
- **Logical formalism**: mistakes internal consistency or visual regularity for fit with the real context.
- **Consultant collage**: accumulates specialist facts without organizing their interactions.

## Worked Example
A vacuum cleaner's materials must balance assembly simplicity, local performance, joint complexity, maintenance, and cost. Each choice helps some demands and harms others. The example shows that even a small object is a network, not a checklist; a city-scale environment makes the same problem vastly larger. The remedy is not a preferred form but a notation for the pattern of conflicts and agreements.

## Key Takeaways
1. Externalize complexity before attempting synthesis.
2. Make assumptions inspectable without pretending the method can select the final form.
3. Judge rationality by whether it improves fit, not whether the result looks orderly.
4. Place personal judgment deliberately instead of letting it enter everywhere invisibly.
