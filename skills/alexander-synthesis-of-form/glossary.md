# Glossary

**Adaptation time** - Expected time for an error-correcting system to reach fit; heavily affected by coupling. (Ch 3)

**Arbitrary characteristic** - A formal commitment not justified by the active requirement set. (Ch 7)

**Binary misfit variable** - A relation represented as `1` when a particular misfit occurs and `0` when it does not. (Ch 2)

**Component** - A bounded unit in a larger pattern and, simultaneously, a pattern organizing smaller units. (Epilogue)

**Concurrence** - An interaction where action against one misfit tends to help eliminate another. (Ch 8)

**Conflict** - An interaction where action against one misfit tends to worsen another. (Ch 8)

**Constructive diagram** - A minimal physical idea that expresses exactly the implications of a coherent requirement set. (Ch 7)

**Context** - Everything outside the controllable form that makes demands on it. (Ch 2)

**Cross-coupling** - Dependencies linking variables placed in different proposed subsystems. (Ch 9)

**Decomposition** - Recursive partitioning of a requirement system into comparatively independent subsets. (Ch 9)

**Ensemble** - Form and context considered together as the locus of fit or misfit. (Ch 2)

**Extensional definition** - Defining a set by its member variables rather than by a verbal concept. (Ch 6)

**Field description** - A unitary account of contextual forces that would simultaneously imply the fitting form. (Ch 2)

**Fit** - Mutual acceptability between form and context; operationally, the absence of selected active misfits. (Ch 2)

**Form** - The part of the ensemble the designer can control. (Ch 2)

**Form diagram** - The aspect of a constructive diagram specific enough to satisfy its requirements but general enough to exclude arbitrary detail. (Ch 7)

**Full subgraph** - The graph on a subset of variables containing every original link whose endpoints both lie in that subset. (App 2)

**Functional hierarchy** - Nested organization of requirement sets produced by decomposition. (Ch 9)

**Hierarchy of sets** - A tree whose children are disjoint and collectively exhaust their parent. (Ch 6)

**Information transfer** - Dependence communicated between subsystems; the formal decomposition seeks to minimize it across boundaries. (App 2)

**Interaction** - A relation where changing prospects for one misfit changes prospects for another. (Ch 8)

**Loss of innocence** - The irreversible move from unexamined intuition to choices whose assumptions can be represented and criticized. (Ch 1)

**Misfit** - A specific, observable failure in the relation between form and context. (Ch 2)

**Pattern-unit duality** - The principle that every component is both a unit and a pattern. (Epilogue)

**Physical hierarchy** - Nested organization of components and patterns in the realized form. (Epilogue)

**Program** - A form-independent structural description of the design problem, expressed as a hierarchy of interacting misfit variables. (Ch 6)

**Requirement diagram** - The aspect of a constructive diagram that includes only features relevant to one requirement set. (Ch 7)

**Scope normalization** - Making variables comparable in breadth and significance before interaction analysis. (Ch 8)

**Selfconscious process** - Specialized, concept-mediated form-making in which designers explicitly invent forms. (Ch 5)

**Signed graph** - A graph whose vertices are misfit variables and whose links encode concurrence or conflict. (Ch 8)

**Subsystem** - An internally coherent set with comparatively weak interaction to the rest of the system. (Ch 3)

**Synthesis** - Invention of physical order in response to function. (Ch 1)

**Unselfconscious process** - Tradition-governed form-making that accumulates local corrections without explicit design theory. (Ch 4)
